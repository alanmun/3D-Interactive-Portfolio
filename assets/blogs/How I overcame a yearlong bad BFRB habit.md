BFRB stands for *Body Focused Reptitive Behavior*—It's basically a term used for when people have some kind of self-grooming type of thing they keep doing to themselves such as biting, pulling, picking, or scraping your own hair or skin or whatever.

### How it all started

about a year ago, around the time I came off some medication I was trying to treat anxiety, I noticed I had started to scratch my scalp whenever I would think hard. It didn't have to be a stressful problem I was solving, anytime I went in deep thought about anything—reading an invigorating article, solving an engineering task at my job, or dwelling on the consequences of choosing some action over another in a video game—I would instinctively and automatically scratch my scalp at a specific spot, which seemed to be my favorite spot for some reason.

in a few months, I noticed a gap, or patch, where hair used to be. *I was starting to dig out my own hair*. I freaked out. At its worst point, it was a dime sized bald spot. I tried:
- asking AI
- going on /r/trichotillomania and /r/dermatology subreddits for help
- buying stupid stress toys to squeeze on and distract my hands with
- buying a bandana and also wearing my hats indoors so I would physically block the hair from being scratched
- hitting myself (lightly slapping) whenever I did it as a positive punishment operant conditioning sort of strategy

nothing worked. I continued to languish from this problem for months. I thought it would never get better. Nearly every day I would pass by the mirror, obsessively stopping to inspect it. I felt defective, and one of the first times I felt like I didn't have control over my own body, like this alter ego of mine could just hijack my body to wreak havoc and then dip out, leaving the conscious version of myself to deal with the aftermath. 

### What finally worked
I went on vacation for 2 weeks with my girlfriend to Europe. We had a wedding to attend and we decided to spin it into a 2 week vacation instead of a 4 day sucky flight to Vienna. It was a great call on our part, and the extended break gave me a lot of time to think. I was embarrassed about the amount of missing hair I had and couldn't take it any longer. I kept cycling through ideas, but I knew I needed to pick something and get it to stick. After chatting with AI (for probably the 9th time), I came up with a simple plan that wasn't too different from what I had done before. 

the plan was simple. I had other BFRBs, but we would just have me focus on eliminating the newest one that was manifesting in actual visible damage to my hair. It only involved following this rule:

> If I reach up and touch my hair without pre-intention, I will stop and snap out of whatever I am doing and try to mentally reset for 15 seconds, entering a sort of timeout where I am only allowed to check in with myself.

(By pre-intention, I mean that touching my hair because I need to adjust some strands so they don't block my vision or something is fine—I had an intentional reason to reach up there. If it's automatic, that's a no go.)

at first, I went into this "time-out" **so** many times while in Vienna. The first few days I implemented it, I would scratch, stop, and go at least 10-20 times per day. But over the rest of the two weeks it lessened and lessened quite quickly. By the last ~4 days, I pretty much never touched my head, maybe once per day if at all. This change was so profound to me.

when I went back to the states, I was afraid the problem would recur. Things had been so peaceful in Europe, it was a period of low responsibility and stress. Would it all come falling down now that I have chores and work to take care of? 

it's been over a month and I'm pleased to say that it hasn't regressed. I still use the technique, but rarely. My scalp has improved, too. I can barely see the patch anymore of thinned/missing hair. In another month or so it should be fully filled in!

### Why did it work?

while in the hotel room in Vienna, I actually suggested punishing myself again like I did before. ChatGPT told me to chill the fuck out and treat it more like a timeout. 

okay, but why did it work? Why didn't it work when I hit myself? I think it was two things working together:

being on vacation for 2 weeks was clearly a fire starter. This totally novel environment gave my mind a break from the seemingly unending routines, the eroding monotony of my life back in the states. Those repeated doomed cycles had left me feeling despondent and unwilling to see a brighter future after so many failed attempts. The fancy hotel room, the beautiful Viennese architecture and these new experiences were inspiring, and I was willing to seriously try something new and believe in it during that time. It instilled in me a desire to just... try again? Try harder? It's hard to return back to that mental state now. 

the main reason is that this new plan, or rule, I've employed:
- **Is kind to myself**: I don't view it as a punishment anymore when I go into time-out. Instead, it is self-care. This makes it something more to look forward to rather than a another energy-costing effort I have to force myself to do.
- **Is an opportunity to break out of whatever mental loop I'm stuck in.** 15 seconds isn't enough time to break a flow state while coding or whatever, it's just enough time to check in with myself. I notice that I often find I am sitting in an uncomfortable way without even noticing it until I go into timeout, that I've forgotten to eat for way too long, that I am cold and should put on more clothing, etc. After I take care of my body's needs, I find that the scalp scratching drops off severely.
- **Is *just* annoying enough to be a penalty to progress without being a punishment.** I have to stop the work I am doing to pause for 15 seconds, which actually makes it a hindrance for me to touch my scalp now when I am working.
